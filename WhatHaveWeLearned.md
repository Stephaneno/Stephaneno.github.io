# What have we learned so far?

*10/31/2026*

---

**Cancer Genome:**


Cancer was historically classified by tissue of origin—breast, lung, or colon. Modern genomic sequencing shifted this idea by reading the precise DNA sequence of tumors, as documented in landmark reviews by Vogelstein et al. (2013) and Garraway & Lander (2013).

**Mountains vs. Hills:**

Sequencing thousands of tumor genomes revealed that the vast majority of genetic alterations are harmless "passenger" mutations. Out of approximately 20,000 genes in the human genome, Vogelstein et al. identified only about 140 "driver genes" capable of driving tumorigenesis—referred to as the "mountains" in the genomic landscape.

---

**Key Terms:**

SNPs : Single-base variations in DNA that account for genetic diversity among individuals, influencing disease susceptibility and drug metabolism.

Oncogenes: Normal growth-regulating genes that acquire gain-of-function mutations, causing continuous cellular proliferation.

---

**Practical Application:**

Through understanding more and more on cancer, we have been able to utilize modern technology to document all of this info, including mutations, cancer patterns, and much more. Through coutlness research and storage we are now able to not only understand individuals on a microscopic level, but sort them very quickly and treat them better than ever with ai databases specialized for this job specifically, making the medical field that much better for patients that already suffered enough.
